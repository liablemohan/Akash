दशाश्वमेध घाट
Dashashwamedh
Ghat
Where Brahma himself performed the Dasha-ashwamedha yajna — the ten-horse sacrifice — to welcome Lord Shiva back to Kashi. Each evening, the Ganga Aarti transforms this stone into a stage for fire and devotion.
मणिकर्णिका घाट
Manikarnika
Ghat
The great cremation ghat, where pyres have burned without pause for three thousand years. To die at Manikarnika is to receive moksha from Shiva's own lips — liberation from the endless cycle of birth and death.
अस्सी घाट
Assi
Ghat
Where the river Assi surrenders herself into the Ganga at the southern boundary of sacred Kashi. The mornings here belong to the bathers and the birds; the evenings to pilgrims circling a Shivalinga beneath a peepal tree.
हरिश्चन्द्र घाट
Harishchandra
Ghat
Named for the legendary king who kept his word at every cost — who worked as a keeper of the dead rather than speak a single untruth. It is the older of Kashi's two burning ghats, and the more solitary.
सिन्धिया घाट
Scindia
Ghat
The tilting Shiva temple, half-submerged in the Ganga, stands as proof that even stone must bow to the river. Built by the Scindia dynasty and swallowed by the flood — still visited, still consecrated, still alive.
